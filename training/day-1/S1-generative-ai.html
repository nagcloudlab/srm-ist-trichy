🧠 How Does a Machine "Create"?

It doesn't imagine like us.
It learns patterns from millions of examples.

Step 1: Training

Show the model 1 million cat photos.
It learns: ears, whiskers, fur, eyes…

Step 2: Generating

"Give me a new cat."
It creates a new image following learned patterns.
This cat never existed!

“How exactly does it learn and create?”

There are different approaches.
Each one solves this differently.

Transformers

Idea: Predict the next word (or pixel) based on everything before it.

Auto-complete on your phone, but much smarter

For images, it predicts the next patch of pixels. For text, the next word.

Pros

Best for text (ChatGPT). Can do images too (DALL-E 1, GPT-4o). Understands context very well.

Cons

Needs massive data. Very large models. Expensive to train.

🧠 Knowledge Check

If diffusion models produce better images than GANs, why hasn't everyone switched?

Click to reveal

Speed. Standard diffusion needs 20–100 denoising steps per image (often seconds). GANs need one forward pass (milliseconds). For real-time applications — video games, live filters, on-device screening — GANs remain a strong choice; the main alternative, few-step distilled diffusion, often borrows an adversarial (GAN) loss to get there.

A GAN has no explicit "target image" to learn from. How can it learn without a right answer?

Click to reveal

Through competition. The Discriminator becomes the "teacher" — it tells the Generator "that's not convincing enough." G doesn't need a target image, just a critic who keeps raising the bar. This is fundamentally different from all other approaches.

ChatGPT can generate images. Is that a GAN, a Transformer, or something else?

Click to reveal

Not a GAN. ChatGPT first used DALL-E 3 — a diffusion model guided by a Transformer text encoder. Since 2025 it generates images natively with GPT-4o, a Transformer. GAN ideas still appear around the edges (e.g. adversarial losses in image tokenizers and distilled samplers). The approaches are blending.
