Why betas=(0.5, 0.999)?

Adam has two momentum values. beta1 controls how much past gradients influence the current step. Default is 0.9, but GANs work better with 0.5. Why? GAN training changes direction a lot — too much momentum overshoots.

Knowledge Check

The training loop is identical to L03. If nothing changed there, why are DCGAN images so much better?

Click to reveal

Because the architecture determines what the network can learn, not the training loop. Conv layers understand spatial structure — they know neighboring pixels are related. The training loop just provides the learning signal (BCE loss, backprop). Better architecture = better capacity to learn realistic patterns.

DCGAN uses no pooling layers (MaxPool, AvgPool). Why replace pooling with stride=2 convolutions?

Click to reveal

Pooling throws away information using a fixed rule (take the max, take the average). Stride=2 convolution lets the network learn how to downsample. Learned downsampling preserves the information the network finds most useful, while pooling might discard exactly the details that matter.

G uses ReLU but D uses LeakyReLU. What would break if you swapped them?

Click to reveal

If D used ReLU, many neurons would "die" (output 0 for all negative inputs) and D would lose gradient information. D needs all gradient signals to judge real vs fake accurately. LeakyReLU(0.2) keeps 20% of negatives alive. G can use ReLU safely because its job (generating) is less sensitive to dead neurons.
