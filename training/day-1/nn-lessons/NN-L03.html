Why we subtract the gradient

The gradient points uphill. We want downhill. So we subtract:

wnew = wold − η × gradient

This process — always moving opposite to the gradient — is called gradient descent.

Left of minimum (w = 1)

gradient = −9.3

w = 1.0 − η × (−9.3) → increases

✔ Moves RIGHT toward 2 (correct direction)

Right of minimum (w = 3)

gradient = +9.3

w = 3.0 − η × (+9.3) → decreases

✔ Moves LEFT toward 2 (correct direction)

Both directions move toward lower loss — that’s gradient descent

The gradient knows direction — but not distance

We know the weight should increase. But by how much?

Learning rateNew weightObservation
Too small (0.01) w: 1.0 → 1.09 Barely moved. Needs ~50 steps.
Just right (0.10) w: 1.0 → 1.93 Almost at 2 in one step!
Too big (0.30) w: 1.0 → 3.79 Overshot! Farther than before.
