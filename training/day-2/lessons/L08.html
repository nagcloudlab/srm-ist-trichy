Unlike JS, W measures how far apart the piles are — even when they never overlap.

Cheapest plan between two small piles: W = 2 · worked example

GIVEN
  • real: ½ at x = 0, ½ at x = 2
  • fake: ½ at x = 1, ½ at x = 5
  1. 1plan A: 1 → 0, 5 → 2  ⇒  ½·1 + ½·3 = 2.0move each fake to the nearer real spot
  2. 2plan B: 1 → 2, 5 → 0  ⇒  ½·1 + ½·5 = 3.0a worse plan
  3. 3W = min(2.0, 3.0) = 2.0inf = take the cheapest plan
  4. 4JS = log 2 ≈ 0.693no overlap, so JS cannot tell 2 from 200
  5. =W = 2.0 — and it shrinks smoothly as the fake pile moves closer
Plan A is optimal: in 1-D, matching piles in sorted order is always cheapest.

A slope-1 critic recovers W = 2 · worked example

GIVEN
  • real: ½ at 0, ½ at 2
  • fake: ½ at 1, ½ at 5

  1. 1Er[f] = −(½·0 + ½·2) = −1.0average real score
  2. 2Eg[f] = −(½·1 + ½·5) = −3.0average fake score

From Discriminator to Critic

Critic vs Discriminator

Not a Classifier Any More

D answers “real or fake?” with a probability.

C is the f in the duality — it outputs a score.

No Sigmoid, No Saturation

Score can be any number. Only differences matter.

−50 → gradient exists
+200 → gradient exists

2

The WGAN Loss

Critic Loss

LC = −E[C(x)] + E[C(G(z))]

C maximizes the gap E[C(x)] − E[C(G(z))] ≈ W  →  we minimize its negative.

In Words

Critic loss = −(average score on real) + (average score on fake)

To minimize this:

  • Make score on real images HIGH (first term more negative)
  • Make score on fake images LOW (second term smaller)

In PyTorch

loss_C = critic(fake).mean() - critic(real).mean()

Generator Loss

LG = −E[C(G(z))]

In Words

Generator loss = −(average Critic score on fakes)

To minimize this: make the Critic give HIGH scores to fakes.

In PyTorch

loss_G = -critic(fake).mean()

Compare

No BCELoss. No labels. No Sigmoid. Just means and minus signs.

Critic and generator loss on one batch · worked example

LC = E[C(G(z))] − E[C(x)]   ·   LG = −E[C(G(z))]
GIVEN
  • C(real) = 2.0, 1.0, 3.0
  • C(fake) = −1.0, 0.0, −2.0
  1. 1E[C(x)] = 2.0  ·  E[C(G(z))] = −1.0mean scores
  2. 2LC = −1.0 − 2.0 = −3.0critic minimizes this
  3. 3W estimate = −LC = 3.0gap between real and fake
  4. 4LG = −(−1.0) = 1.0G pushes fake scores up
  5. 5later: mean C(fake) = 1.5 → LC = −0.5fakes improved: W estimate falls to 0.5
  6. =−LC tracks W and falls toward 0 as G improves
No sigmoid, no labels, no logs — just means and a minus sign.

The Lipschitz Constraint

The Speed Limit

|C(x1) − C(x2)| ≤ ‖x1 − x2‖

The Critic's output can't "jump" — it must change smoothly.

Without Speed Limit

C(real) = +1,000,000
C(fake) = −1,000,000

Scale C by 10 → gap ×10. The max is ∞ — no distance at all.

With Speed Limit (1-Lipschitz)

C(real) = +3.2
C(fake) = −1.5

Gap is bounded. Its max over such C is W (the duality).

Weight Clipping

The Idea

After every Critic update, clip all weights to [−c, c].

param.data.clamp_(-0.01, 0.01)

Bounded weights → C is K-Lipschitz for some K → we estimate K·W.

Scaled distance — still fine for gradients.

Paper Settings (Arjovsky et al. 2017)

  • Clip c = 0.01
  • ncritic = 5 critic steps per G step
  • RMSProp, lr = 5e-5
  • Batch size 64

The authors call clipping “clearly a terrible way” to enforce Lipschitz — L09 replaces it.

Training Differences

Train the Critic MORE (5:1)

BCE GAN (1:1)

Train D once → Train G once
Train D once → Train G once

D too strong = bad (signal becomes useless)

WGAN (5:1)

Train C five times → Train G once
Train C five times → Train G once

C closer to optimal = better W estimate & gradient

You WANT the Critic to be powerful. This is the opposite of standard GANs.

Use RMSProp, Not Adam

Why?

  • Paper: with Adam (momentum), WGAN training sometimes became unstable
  • The critic’s target keeps moving — a non-stationary problem
  • RMSProp: per-weight step size, no momentum — behaves well there

In PyTorch

opt_C = torch.optim.RMSprop(critic.parameters(), lr=5e-5)
opt_G = torch.optim.RMSprop(gen.parameters(), lr=5e-5)

WGAN-GP (L09) brings Adam back — with β1 = 0 (no momentum).

The WGAN Training Loop

opt_C = torch.optim.RMSprop(critic.parameters(), lr=5e-5)
opt_G = torch.optim.RMSprop(gen.parameters(),    lr=5e-5)

for real, _ in loader:
    real = real.to(device); bs = real.size(0)

    # ── Critic: n_critic = 5 steps ──
    for _ in range(5):
        z = torch.randn(bs, z_dim, device=device)
        fake = gen(z).detach()
        loss_C = critic(fake).mean() - critic(real).mean()
        opt_C.zero_grad(); loss_C.backward(); opt_C.step()
        for p in critic.parameters():
            p.data.clamp_(-0.01, 0.01)      # ← weight clipping

    # ── Generator: 1 step ──
    z = torch.randn(bs, z_dim, device=device)
    loss_G = -critic(gen(z)).mean()
    opt_G.zero_grad(); loss_G.backward(); opt_G.step()

What Weight Clipping Gets Wrong

Capacity Loss

Weights pile up at −0.01 / +0.01. Critic learns overly simple functions.

Sensitive to c

c = 0.001 → gradients vanish. c = 0.1 → gradients explode.

Slow Convergence

Large c: critic slow to reach optimum. Training is slow overall.

The fix? Replace weight clipping with Gradient Penalty — that's L09 (WGAN-GP).
