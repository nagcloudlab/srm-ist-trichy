Evaluating GANs

Unit 2 made GANs stable and steerable. Now: which one is actually better?

The Problem

"It looks good to me" is not a scientific answer.

We need numbers.

Why Evaluation Is Hard

Image Classifier

  • 10,000 labeled test images
  • Compare prediction vs truth
  • Accuracy = 94.2%
  • Done.
VS

GAN

  • No "correct answer"
  • A generated face isn't right or wrong
  • Quality is a spectrum
  • ???

What Does "Good" Mean?

1. High Quality (Fidelity)

Each image looks realistic. Sharp, coherent, no artifacts.

2. High Variety (Diversity)

Images cover the full range. Not one face over and over.

3. Matches Real Data

Distribution of fakes matches distribution of reals.

One perfect face

High quality, zero diversity

Blurry random noise

High diversity, zero quality

We need to measure BOTH.

Bad Approaches (That People Still Use)

Eye Test

  • Subjective
  • Not scalable (50 models?)
  • Cherry-picking (best samples only)
  • Can't detect mode collapse

Discriminator Loss

  • BCE D loss hovers near 2 ln 2 ≈ 1.386 at balance — good or bad images
  • WGAN loss is better but still not a quality metric
  • Low D loss =/= good images

Per-Pixel Comparison

  • Generated images shouldn't match reals (that's memorization!)
  • Pixel distance ignores perception
  • Shift 1px = big distance, same face

We need something smarter.

1

Inception Score (IS)

Inception Score — How It Works

Use Inception v3 (trained on ImageNet) to judge generated images.

Step-by-Step

  1. Generate 50,000 images from G
  2. Feed each through Inception v3
  3. Get class probabilities for each
  4. Compute score from confidence + diversity

Good Quality

"92% dog, 3% cat, 2% car..."
Confident = sharp image

Bad Quality

"11% dog, 10% cat, 9% car..."
Uncertain = blurry/ambiguous

Diversity Check

All images = "dog"? Low diversity.
Spread across classes? High diversity.

IS Formula & Score Range

IS = exp( E_x[ KL( p(y|x) || p(y) ) ] )

In Words

p(y|x) = what Inception thinks about ONE image (should be confident)

p(y) = what Inception thinks about ALL images (should be spread)

High KL = high quality AND high diversity

Problems with Inception Score

1. Never sees real data

GAN generates perfect dogs but trained on cats? IS says "great!"

2. Limited to ImageNet

For MNIST or faces, Inception doesn't have relevant classes

3. Misses intra-class collapse

10 classes with only 1 example each? IS doesn't notice

4. Sensitive to details

Sample count, splits, resizing all affect the score

FID — The Standard

Frechet Inception Distance

Why features, not pixels?

Pixels: position-sensitive, low-level
Features: capture WHAT is in the image, not WHERE

How FID Compares Features

FID treats features as two multivariate Gaussian distributions:

Real: mean = mu_r, covariance = Sigma_r

Fake: mean = mu_f, covariance = Sigma_f

FID = ||mu_r - mu_f||^2 + Tr(Sigma_r + Sigma_f - 2*sqrt(Sigma_r * Sigma_f))

Term 1: Center Distance

"Do fakes have similar average features to reals?"

Term 2: Shape Distance

"Do fakes have similar feature variety to reals?"

FID = center distance + shape distance

Fréchet Inception Distance · term by term

FID = ‖μr − μg‖² + Tr( Σr + Σg − 2 (ΣrΣg)½ )

Why FID is the standard

  • Compares fake to real
  • Captures quality AND diversity
  • Works on most image domains (features come from ImageNet)
  • Reproducible — if sample count + preprocessing are fixed
  • Correlates with human judgment

Practical Concerns

Sample Count

Few images: FID biased upward

10,000: decent

50,000: gold standard

Preprocessing

Resize to 299x299

Same processing for real + fake

Different resizing can shift FID by several points

FID Limitations

Assumes Gaussian features

Biased with small samples

Can't tell you WHY images are bad

Knowledge Check

Two GANs both score FID = 20. One is blurry but covers all 10 digits; the other is sharp but only draws 6 digits. Can FID tell them apart?

Click to reveal

No — one number mixes quality and diversity. Precision and recall separate them: blurry-but-diverse = low precision, high recall; sharp-but-collapsed = high precision, low recall.

If FID uses Inception (trained on ImageNet), does it make sense to compute FID for MNIST digits? Why or why not?

Click to reveal

It still works! Inception's internal features (pool3 layer) capture general visual patterns — edges, shapes, textures — not just ImageNet classes. The 2048-dim features are semantic enough to distinguish "good digits" from "bad digits" even though Inception was never trained on digits. It's not perfect: many MNIST papers instead use features from an MNIST-trained classifier.

Your competitor reports FID = 20 using 1,000 images. You get FID = 30 using 50,000 images. Who actually has the better GAN?

Click to reveal

You can't tell from these numbers — and the competitor may be better. Small samples bias FID upward, so their 20 would likely drop further with 50,000 images. Always compare at the same sample count (and same preprocessing).
