Lesson 14

GAN Bias and Fairness

What happens when a "good" GAN learns the wrong things?

The Hidden Problem

FID = 15
"Great score!"

But look closer...

  • 85% of outputs are light-skinned
  • 90% are young adults
  • Dark-skinned faces are blurrier
  • Older faces have more artifacts

The overall FID hid the problem.

What Is Bias in GANs?

Bias = outputs don't fairly represent the diversity of the real world

Level 1: Data Bias

Training data is unbalanced.

Face dataset: 80% light skin, 20% dark skin. The GAN learns this imbalance.

Level 2: Model Bias

The GAN amplifies the imbalance.

Data: 80/20 split. GAN output can drift to e.g. 95/5 (illustrative). Minority gets even less.

Level 3: Evaluation Bias

FID doesn't catch the problem.

Majority group looks great, minority's poor quality is averaged away.

How Bias Enters Through Data

CelebA Dataset (most-used)

  • ≈ 58% labelled “female”
  • Majority light-skinned (no skin-tone labels)
  • ≈ 77% labelled “young”
  • Posed, made-up celebrity photos

GANs trained on CelebA

  • Skew toward light-skinned, young faces
  • Dark-skinned faces are less detailed
  • Older faces are rare and blurry

The Amplification Effect (illustrative)

Data: 70% A, 30% B
GAN: 85% A, 15% B

G gets MORE gradient signal from group A → gets better at A → focuses even more on A. Reported for face GANs: Jain et al., 2020.

Vicious cycle.

Mode Collapse = Bias

Face Dataset

GAN collapses to 5 face types out of 50.

Which 5 survive? The majority groups.

Which 45 disappear? The minority groups.

Mode collapse disproportionately erases underrepresented groups.

In MNIST Terms

If 3 digits collapse (a plausible pattern, not a rule):

Survive: "easy" ones (1, 0, 7)

Collapse: "hard" ones (8, 9, 4)

Not random — structural bias in which modes survive.

Why This Matters

1. Synthetic Training Data

GAN generates training images for face detector → mostly light-skinned → detector fails on dark-skinned faces → real-world discrimination

2. Medical Augmentation

GAN generates synthetic X-rays from one hospital population → model works poorly on other populations

3. Creative Tools

AI art trained on Western art → default output reflects Western aesthetics → other styles stereotyped

4. Deepfakes

Biased face GANs create better fakes of majority faces → detection tools can be worse at catching fakes of minorities

Next: how to detect and fix it.

Detecting Bias

Step 1: Check Your Data

Count examples per group.

Class 0: 5,923 ( 9.9%)
Class 1: 6,742 (11.2%)
Class 8: 5,851 ( 9.8%)
...

For faces: check age, gender, skin tone distributions.

Step 2: Per-Group FID

Compute FID per group — with the same N per group (small N biases FID up, L13).

Class 0: FID = 15.2
Class 1: FID = 12.8
Class 7: FID = 14.1
Class 8: FID = 45.3
Class 9: FID = 38.7

Classes 8, 9 are much worse! (illustrative)

Step 3: Visual Inspection

Generate 100 images per group. Ask:

  • Some groups blurrier?
  • Some less diverse?
  • Some have artifacts?
  • Stereotypical patterns?

Mitigation Strategies

1. Balance the Data

Oversample minority groups or undersample majority.

Pros: simple. Cons: oversampling can overfit.

2. Conditional Training (cGAN)

Give each group its own label. G learns each separately.

Each group gets dedicated gradient signal.

3. Loss Weighting

Give minority groups higher loss weight.

young: weight=0.2 (80% data)
old: weight=0.8 (20% data)

4. Per-Group Evaluation

Don't just compute one FID. Compute per group.

Overall FID = 22 (great!) but Group C FID = 52 (terrible!).

5. Diverse Data Collection

The best mitigation is prevention. Collect data that represents the population. No algorithm can fully fix biased data.

Fairness Metrics

MetricWhat It Checks
Per-Group FIDAll groups should have similar FIDs
Demographic ParityGAN generates each group equally often
Quality ParityEach group's images are equally realistic
CoverageAll subgroups within each group are covered

The Tension

Rebalance data to 50/50 → FID goes UP

Why? Real data was 80/20, fake is now 50/50. Distributions don't match!

The "best" FID comes from copying biases exactly.

A fair GAN may have a higher FID. That's OK.

Deployment Checklist

1. What groups exist in my data?

2. How balanced is the representation?

3. Have I computed per-group FID?

4. Are some groups blurrier or less diverse?

5. What will this GAN be used for?

6. Who could be harmed by biased outputs?

7. Have I documented the known biases?

8. Have I tried mitigation strategies?

Lesson 14 Summary

1

GANs Amplify Bias

They don't just copy data imbalances — they make them worse.

2

FID Hides Bias

Overall FID looks great while minority groups suffer. Always check per-group.

3

Prevention > Cure

Diverse data collection beats any algorithmic fix. Document known limitations.

Knowledge Check

Your GAN has FID = 12 (excellent). But when you compute per-group FID, Group C scores 52. How is this possible, and what does it tell you?

Click to reveal

Overall FID is computed on the pooled features of all groups. If Group C is a small minority (say 5% of data), its poor quality barely moves the pooled mean and covariance. The majority groups' low FIDs dominate the average. This is exactly why per-group FID is essential — overall FID is blind to minority-group problems.

You rebalance your dataset to 50/50 between two groups. FID goes from 15 to 25. Your manager says "you made the GAN worse!" How do you respond?

Click to reveal

FID measures how well fake matches real distributions. The real data is 80/20, but now fakes are 50/50 — so they don't match the real distribution, causing higher FID. But the GAN is actually FAIRER. Show per-group FID: both groups now score similarly, whereas before Group B scored much worse. A higher overall FID for a fairer model is a feature, not a bug.

Someone says "bias is a data problem, not a model problem." Why is this only half right?

Click to reveal

The data IS biased, but GANs can AMPLIFY the bias (e.g. 80/20 drifting toward 95/5). The Generator gets more gradient signal from majority groups, gets better at them, focuses even more on them — a vicious cycle that makes the model bias WORSE than the data bias. Plus, evaluation bias (using overall FID) hides the problem. It's a pipeline problem: data + model + evaluation all contribute.
