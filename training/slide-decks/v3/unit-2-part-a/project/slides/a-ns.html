Part A · §4 · What “non-saturating” means

Saturating vs non-saturating

Original: log(1 − D(G(z)))

When D(fake) → 0, its gradient becomes very small. That is saturation.

Non-saturating: −log D(G(z))

∂L/∂a = D(fake) − 1. At D(fake) = 0.001 it is ≈ −0.999: still strong.

a is the discriminator’s logit, its raw score before the sigmoid. The original loss’s push is −D(fake), almost zero when D rejects fakes; the non-saturating push stays near −1.
