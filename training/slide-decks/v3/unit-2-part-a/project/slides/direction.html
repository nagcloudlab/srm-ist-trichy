Part A · §5 · Key idea

Gradient size survives. Direction can still be useless.

A near-perfect D is almost flat around the data, so ∂a/∂x → 0, and the updates it does give are noisy (Arjovsky & Bottou, 2017).

This sharpens the notes. Arjovsky and Bottou showed that as D approaches optimal on separated distributions, its gradient with respect to the input vanishes, and with the -log D loss the generator updates become unstable with growing variance. Now let us see why, by looking at what the GAN is really measuring.
