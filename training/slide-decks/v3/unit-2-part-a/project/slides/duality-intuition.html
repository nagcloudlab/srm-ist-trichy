Part A · §14 · The critic as a hill

Think of the critic as a hill

Real data on high ground, fake in the valley.

The slope can never exceed 1, so the height gap can never exceed the distance.

Steepest legal ramp: height gained = W = 10. G simply walks uphill.

The duality formula scares people, so give it a picture. The critic assigns a height to every point. It wants real points high and fake points low, so the height gap is as big as possible. The Lipschitz rule says no slope steeper than 1. With real at 0 and fake at 10, the biggest gap is a straight ramp of slope 1, and the height gained is exactly 10, the Wasserstein distance. That is all the sup over 1-Lipschitz f means. The generator then moves its samples uphill along this ramp, which is why WGAN gives a useful direction even when real and fake do not overlap.
