Part A · New tool · KL in one slide

KL: how surprised is the fake model?

KL(real ‖ fake) = average over real x of log( real(x) / fake(x) )

In words: how surprised is the fake model by real data?

At x = 0: real = 1, fake = 0

log(1 / 0) = ∞

Habit 1: no overlap → KL blows up.

Habit 2: one-sided: KL(real‖fake) ≠ KL(fake‖real).

Verdict

KL fails exactly in our picture. We need something safer.

Keep KL brief. It averages, over real samples, the log of how much more likely the real data makes x than the fake model does. In our picture the real pile sits at 0, where the fake model puts zero probability, so the ratio is 1 / 0 and the log is infinite. That is habit one: with no overlap KL blows up, and in GANs the piles often do not overlap. Habit two: it is one-sided; swapping the two arguments gives a different number. Both habits rule KL out as our difference number.
