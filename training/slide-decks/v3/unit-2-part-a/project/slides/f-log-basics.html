Recall · Unit 1

A quick warm-up so no one is lost later. Log of 1 is 0. Log of anything between 0 and 1 is negative, and it plunges toward minus infinity as x approaches 0. So minus log of a probability is a positive number that is small when the probability is near 1 and huge when it is near 0. That is exactly the shape we want for a loss. The second rule explains why logs appear everywhere in probability: independent probabilities multiply, logs turn products into sums, which are easier to average and differentiate.
