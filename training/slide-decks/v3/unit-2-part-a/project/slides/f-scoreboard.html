Recall · Unit 1

Start here, with almost no formula. One scoreboard, V, shows how well the discriminator is doing. D wants it high, G wants it low, because a low score means D is being fooled. The two dots are the two cases from D's loss slide: a sharp D had loss 0.33, so V = −0.33, near the top; a fooled D had loss 2.81, so V = −2.81, near the bottom. V is never above 0; 0 would be a perfect D. Ask the class: when G gets better, which way does the score move? Down.
