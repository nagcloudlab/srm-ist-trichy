Part A · §9 · Why this hurts learning

JS is flat: it can’t tell 10 from 1

Flat means slope = 0: no “how far”, no “which way”. G has nothing to follow.

The same story as a picture. Horizontal axis: how far the fake is from the real data. The orange line is JS: flat at 0.693 for every distance, and 0 only when the fake sits exactly on the real data. A flat curve has zero slope, so gradient descent on JS gives G no signal. The dashed line is what we want instead: a number that shrinks smoothly as G gets closer. Chapter 8 gives it a name: Wasserstein.
