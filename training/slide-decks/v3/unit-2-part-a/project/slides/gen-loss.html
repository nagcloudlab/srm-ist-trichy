L_G = 10 → 5 → 2 → 0: it tracks the distance.

Unlike JS

The critic’s slope never goes flat: at every distance it says “go left, toward real”.

Fix the critic at its best, C(x) = −x, and let the generator move its fake. The real-data term does not depend on G, so G only minimises minus the mean fake score: it wants its fakes to score higher. At 10 the fake scores −10, so L_G = 10; at 5, L_G = 5; at 2, L_G = 2; on top of the real data, 0. The loss now tracks the distance, exactly what JS could not do. And the critic’s slope is −1 everywhere, so G always gets a clear direction. In real training the critic is re-trained between generator steps, but the picture holds.
