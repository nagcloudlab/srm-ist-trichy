Part A · §26 · Gradient norm example

The slope is just an arrow’s length

∇C = [0.6, 0.8]

Across 0.6, up 0.8: the arrow is the hypotenuse.

‖∇C‖ = √(0.6² + 0.8²) = 1

Just right. In 1D the norm is simply |slope|: dC/dx = 4 → norm 4, too steep.

The gradient of the critic is an arrow: it points in the direction where the score rises fastest, and its length says how fast. The norm is that length, Pythagoras in 2D: across 0.6, up 0.8, length 1. For an image the arrow has one component per pixel, thousands of them, but the length is still the square root of the sum of squares. In one dimension the arrow has one component and its length is just the absolute slope.
