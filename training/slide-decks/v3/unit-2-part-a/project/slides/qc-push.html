Quick check: push and direction

a

D(fake) = 0.01. Push from the original loss?

log(1 − D) → push = D(fake)

About 0.01: almost no push.

b

Same D(fake). Push from the flipped loss?

−log D → push = 1 − D(fake)

About 0.99: a strong push.

c

Do the two losses want a different result?

where is each loss smallest?

No. Both want D(fake) → 1. Only the push differs.

d

D is flat at the fake: slope 0.0005. Does the flipped loss save G?

what G feels = push × slope

No. 0.99 × 0.0005 ≈ 0.0005. Strong push, no direction.

Reveal one answer at a time. a and b: the push is the size of the gradient on D's logit, D(fake) for the original loss and 1 − D(fake) for the flipped one. c: both losses are smallest at D(fake) = 1, so the goal is the same. d is the key one: the flipped loss fixes the push, but the chain rule multiplies it by D's slope at the fake. A flat D still starves G. That is why the rest of Part A changes the judge itself.
