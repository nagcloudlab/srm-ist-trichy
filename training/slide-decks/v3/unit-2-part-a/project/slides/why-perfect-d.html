Why does a better D hurt G?

The logic

  1. G’s gradient = push × slope of D’s score.
  2. A near-perfect D has a flat score under the fakes.
  3. Big push × tiny slope = tiny, unreliable direction.

Key idea

The non-saturating loss fixes the push, not the slope. A perfect D has no slope where the fakes are.

Both rows use the non-saturating loss with real at 2 and fake at 8. Unsure D: score a = 0.5(5 − x), D(fake) = 0.18, push 1 − D = 0.82, slope 0.5, gradient 0.41. Near-perfect D: score a = 10·tanh(2(5 − x)), D(fake) ≈ 0.00005, push ≈ 1, slope ≈ 0.0005, gradient ≈ 0.0005. Push and slope multiply: fixing the push alone cannot rescue G.
